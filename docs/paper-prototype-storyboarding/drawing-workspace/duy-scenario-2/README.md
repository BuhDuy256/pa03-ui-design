## Rules that do not bend

- Coloured pens on clean **unlined** paper. **Not pencil.**
- Black = frames and text · Green = ● running, ✓ in range · Red = deviations, alerts,
  FACILITATOR headers · Blue = ♫ music and audio cues.
- **All 15 sheets are the same phone rectangle** ≈ 9 × 16 cm — 2A included.
  *(2A was watch-shaped until 10/08/2026; the strap stubs are gone.)*
- Pace is the biggest thing on any running screen.
- **Label the back of every sheet with its code** (`2A-3`, `2C-4`…) before moving on.
- The Haptic Cue Card and F-2A go in a **separate pile**, `FACILITATOR` in red.
- Nothing on this paper is testing data. The Cue Interpretation Accuracy table stays empty.

## Why this scenario survived the cut

Dr. Duy raised haptic and ambient cue perceptibility in the PA2 peer review, and the team
committed in writing to testing it early. The **Cue Interpretation Accuracy** table — too
fast / too slow / in range, per participant — is the direct answer to that critique.

Which is why the answer must never be visible. 2A Sheets 2 and 4 have **blank fronts**, with
their state on the back. If a participant reads "TOO FAST" they are not interpreting a cue,
and the measurement is gone for every remaining trial with that person.

## 2A — Haptic Pace Feedback

*Feedback through **vibration patterns** — a channel that needs no attention at all.*

### Draw these two first, then put them out of reach

- [ ] **Haptic Cue Card** *(facilitator)* — the answer key

      | State | Vibration | Meaning |
      | --- | --- | --- |
      | In range | `•` | Keep current pace |
      | Too fast | `• •` | Slow down |
      | Too slow | `———` | Speed up |

- [ ] **F-2A** *(facilitator)* Cue repeats, pace still off — watch showing `5:25/km`,
      `▮ ▮ (repeat)`. Use when the participant corrects in the **wrong direction** or does
      not react. Repeat the **same** pattern once. Do not explain. Record: first reading,
      direction moved, second reading.

**F-2A exists because** every one of Sheets 1–5 assumes the runner reads the cue correctly.
A wrong guess is the *expected* result and the most valuable data 2A produces. Without this
card you will improvise, most likely by explaining — which kills the measurement for the rest
of that session.

### Participant sheets — phone rectangles

> **2A is a phone now.** The pulses come from a phone on an armband or in a pocket, not a
> watch on the wrist — a weaker, less localised sensation, which makes the interpretation task
> harder. Put that in **WEAKNESSES / OPEN QUESTIONS** on the card; it strengthens the write-up
> rather than weakening it. Deliver the pulses by holding a vibrating phone against their
> upper arm, out of sight.

- [ ] **2A-1** Normal Running — ● RUNNING, 6:00/km, ♫ MUSIC, "Keep pace"
- [ ] **2A-2** Too Fast · **TWO-SIDED** — front: `5:30/km` **only**
      · back: `FACILITATOR / STATE: TOO FAST / CUE: • • / EXPECTED: slows down`
- [ ] **2A-3** Runner Slows — 6:05/km, ✓, IN RANGE
- [ ] **2A-4** Too Slow · **TWO-SIDED** — front: `6:40/km` **only**
      · back: `FACILITATOR / STATE: TOO SLOW / CUE: ——— / EXPECTED: speeds up`
- [ ] **2A-5** Back in Range — 6:02/km, ✓ IN RANGE, ♫ MUSIC

**Watch for:** the fronts of 2A-2 and 2A-4 carry **nothing but the pace**. No `TOO FAST`, no
pulse marks. Both are answers to the only question 2A asks.

**Deliver the pulses out of sight** — under the table, behind the wrist. If they can watch
your hand, they are reading the hand, not the pattern, and you blanked those fronts for
nothing. **Record their interpretation before turning the sheet over.**

### Storyboard

- [ ] **2A-F1** Context — runner, watch on wrist, eyes forward
      → *Bình runs with the watch on his wrist and his eyes on the road.*
- [ ] **2A-F2** Trigger — watch enlarged, two vibration arcs each side
      → *Two short pulses on his wrist.*
- [ ] **2A-F3** Response — runner with a "?" thought bubble, beginning to slow
      → *He works out what the pattern means. Nobody tells him.*
- [ ] **2A-F4** Outcome — watch showing ✓, head still facing forward
      → *He settles back into range without once looking at the screen.*

- [ ] **2A card** — explanation card, all seven fields.
      Put the open question in WEAKNESSES: *can users tell the patterns apart unaided?*

## 2B — Beat-to-Pace Matching

*Feedback through **music rhythm** — the runner matches an unchanging beat.*

- [ ] **2B-1** Target Setup — Target Pace 6:00/km, Target Cadence `[SET]`, `[START]`
- [ ] **2B-2** Running to the Beat — ♫ MUSIC, `♪ ♪ ♪ ♪`, "Beat = cadence", 6:00/km
- [ ] **2B-3** Pace Too Fast — 5:35/km, "♫ Beat unchanged", "Your steps are ahead"
- [ ] **2B-4** Match Rhythm — two rows of `👟♪  👟♪` **evenly spaced**, ✓ ON RHYTHM
- [ ] **2B-5** Stable Pace — 6:01/km, ✓ Target, ♫ MUSIC

**Wizard-of-Oz, required:**
- On **2B-2** start clapping a steady beat and **keep it going**. This is the reference they
  must have in their ears before 2B-3 means anything.
- On **2B-3** **keep clapping at exactly the same tempo** while saying their pace has
  increased. The gap between their steps and your unchanged beat *is* the feedback.

**Watch for:** 2B-3 used to read "Target beat shown". A beat is heard, not shown, and the line
gave you no action to perform — so the mechanism 2B is built on never happened in the room.
It also implied the beat moves, which inverts the idea: the beat is the **fixed reference**.

### Storyboard

- [ ] **2B-F1** Context — footfalls aligned under evenly spaced beat marks
      → *Bình runs in time with the beat.*
- [ ] **2B-F2** Trigger — footfalls drawn **ahead** of the beat marks
      → *His steps drift ahead of the beat.*
- [ ] **2B-F3** Response — beat marks unchanged, the gap drawn in red
      → *The beat does not move. The gap is the feedback.*
- [ ] **2B-F4** Outcome — footfalls realigned, ✓
      → *He falls back onto the beat. Pace corrected by ear alone.*

- [ ] **2B card** — explanation card, all seven fields

## 2C — Just-in-Time Pace Alert

*Same feedback, different **timing rule** — fires only after deviation persists.*

> **Haptic-primary.** The alert *is* the vibration. The screen is only what the runner would
> see **if** they looked, and they are never asked to. A visual alert would fail Scenario 2's
> own success condition.
>
> **Keep the stopwatch on your side of the table.** A participant watching a counter climb is
> predicting the alert instead of judging whether it arrived at a reasonable moment.
>
> **Sheets 1–3 look nearly identical on purpose.** The alert deliberately *not* firing is the
> whole prototype.

- [ ] **2C-1** In Range — 6:00/km, ✓ IN RANGE, ♫ MUSIC
- [ ] **2C-2** Slightly Too Fast — 5:50/km, "Slightly too fast", "No alert yet"
- [ ] **2C-3** Still Too Fast — 5:45/km, "Deviation continues…", "Waiting for feedback"
- [ ] **2C-4** Just-in-Time Alert — 5:45/km, `▮▮▮ SLOW DOWN`, ♫ MUSIC
- [ ] **2C-5** Back in Range — 6:02/km, ✓ IN RANGE, ♫ MUSIC

**Timing discipline:** start the stopwatch on 2C-2. On 2C-4, **deliver the vibration first,
then hand over the sheet** — 20–30 s after the deviation began. Use the **same delay for
every participant** or their answers about timing cannot be compared.

### Storyboard

- [ ] **2C-F1** Context — runner on target, quiet screen
      → *Bình is on target pace. Nothing is happening — and that is correct.*
- [ ] **2C-F2** Trigger — pace slipping, a timer starting, no alert
      → *Pace slips. The system says nothing yet.*
- [ ] **2C-F3** Response — timer further along, one pulse with vibration arcs
      → *Only after the drift holds does a single pulse arrive.*
- [ ] **2C-F4** Outcome — runner corrected, ✓
      → *He corrects once — and was interrupted once.*

- [ ] **2C card** — explanation card, all seven fields.
      The DIFFERS field must say: 2C changes **when** a pace alert fires; 1C changes what
      the runner receives immediately when a non-critical notification arrives.
