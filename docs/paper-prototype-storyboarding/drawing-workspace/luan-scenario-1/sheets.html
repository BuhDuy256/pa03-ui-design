1B — Between-Track Delivery

How it differs: nothing is blocked and nothing changes form — the interruption waits for the seam between two tracks. 1C shrinks the message to protect the music; 1B moves when it lands.

⚠ 1B was redesigned on 12/08/2026. The old 1B was Adaptive Music (music tempo follows pace). It was cut because none of its five sheets contained an interruption at all, so it could not be compared with 1A and 1C on the one thing Scenario 1 measures — and it inverted Duy's 2B. Any sheet with Cadence ___ spm or HOLD TEMPO on it is the old design. Rationale: Change 18 in PA3-9-Paper-Prototypes-EN.md.

Explanation card

Prototype
1B — Between-Track Delivery
Problem
A notification arriving mid-song cuts into the music the runner is using to hold rhythm.
Motivation
The damage is done by when the message lands, not by the message itself. Move it to a moment where no music is lost and nothing has to be blocked or shortened.
How it works
1. A non-critical notification arrives mid-track and is queued, music untouched · 2. It is released in the seam between two tracks and delivered in full · 3. A critical call bypasses the rule and arrives immediately
Differs
Nothing is blocked and nothing changes form — the interruption waits for the seam between two tracks. 1A changes whether it gets through; 1C changes what it is made of; 1B changes when it lands. Not 2C: 2C delays on how long the runner's pace has been off (body data); 1B delays on where the music is (content structure).
Strengths
Music is never cut · the message arrives whole, unlike a tone · nothing to configure before the run · completes UC-02 by showing a delivery that does no damage
Weaknesses
A long track means a long wait, and time-sensitive messages arrive late · needs music with clear track boundaries — fails on podcasts or continuous mixes · the "waiting" badge may be read as blocked or lost

1C — Ambient Audio Cue

How it differs: interruptions still happen, but arrive as a short tone instead of a voice. 1C is about what a cue is made of — Duy's 2C is the one about when a cue fires. Keep them apart or a grader will read them as duplicates.

Agree the exact sound before the first session — two taps on the table, a finger click, one chime — and use the identical sound every time. A cue that varies between participants cannot be compared across them.

Explanation card

Prototype
1C — Ambient Audio Cue
Problem
Voice coaching interrupts music and breaks rhythm.
Motivation
The interruption is the voice, not the information. A tone carries the same signal at a fraction of the cost.
How it works
1. Pace drifts · 2. One short non-verbal tone plays over unbroken music · 3. It repeats identically rather than escalating
Differs
Changes what the cue is made of — a tone instead of a voice. 2C changes when a cue fires; the two are not variants of each other.
Strengths
Music never ducks · far shorter than speech · language-independent
Weaknesses
Meaning must be learned · may be missed under loud music · carries less detail than speech
