## 2. Day-by-day

### Day 1 — Decide, then draw

**Hour 1 is recruitment.** Message P01–P07 before anything else. They were interviewed about the *problem space* in PA2 and have never seen a prototype, so they satisfy *"no prior knowledge about the prototype you will test."* This is your warm list — use it. PA2's retro named scheduling as the root cause of your last sprint's slippage; this hour is the fix.

**Then ratify the 2 main scenarios** before a single sheet is drawn — the report structure and the slides both hang off this.

Proposed framing (your call to accept or change):

- **Main Scenario 1 — "The uninterrupted solo run"**
  Runner sets up, starts, and completes a music-driven solo run without flow being broken.
  Alternatives: **1A / 1B / 1C** (music flow) and **3A / 3B / 3C** (in-run control)
- **Main Scenario 2 — "Eyes-free correction mid-run"**
  Runner drifts off target pace and corrects without looking at a screen.
  Alternatives: **2A / 2B / 2C**

This satisfies "at least 2 main scenarios" *and* keeps ≥3 alternatives per identified problem, so all 9 prototypes stay in scope.

Rest of the day: specs → draw 45 sheets → draw 36 storyboard frames → review explanation cards. Three people in parallel, ~3 h each.

### Day 2 — Record, then test

Morning: videos (45 min each, in parallel), then upload **unlisted** and verify every link in an incognito window. A private video is an unwatchable video and R1 is 45% of the grade.

Midday: finalize the testing plan, then dry-run the full protocol on a teammate and time it. Cut whatever overruns.

Evening: **the sessions.** Run two facilitator pairs in parallel (facilitator + note-taker each) so 3 participants fit in ~2 hours wall clock instead of 3.5 sequential.

Per participant, ~65 min: 5 min briefing → 5 min background questions → 9 tasks × ~5 min → 10 min wrap-up.

**Priority if you run out of time:** cover **2A / 2B / 2C** fully, without exception. Dr. Duy's PA2 feedback was specifically that haptic/ambient patterns are perceptually hard and need extensive testing, and your group's written response committed to testing them early. Capture the **Cue Interpretation Accuracy** table (too fast / too slow / in range) for 2A and 1C. Landing that one table is worth more at the presentation than thin coverage of all nine.

Never explain what a vibration pattern means before the participant guesses. The guess *is* the data.

Type up notes the same night.

### Day 3 — Analyse, build, rehearse

Morning: analysis from real notes → comparison table → select the best prototype with evidence, named weaknesses, and a concrete improvement.

Midday: draw and record the improved version.

Afternoon: report drafts in parallel (M1 on Paper Prototype, M3 on Formative Testing) while CO builds slides.

Evening: rehearse to 8 minutes. Backfill the weekly report from the meetings you actually held on Days 1–3 — log them as they happen, don't reconstruct them.

Put video links **on the slides as QR codes** and in the report's first page as text links.

## 5. Integrity line — do not cross

AI can write specs, captions, scripts, plan prose, and assemble reports from your material.

AI must **not** generate: observation notes, participant quotes, feedback, meeting minutes, or peer-review comments. Those are empirical records. Generating them is fabrication, not efficiency — and the fabricated version is exactly what a lecturer who has run these sessions himself will notice.

The original plan already drew this line correctly. Keep it.
