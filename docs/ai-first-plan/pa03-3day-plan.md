## 2. Day-by-day

### Day 1 — Decide, then draw

**Hour 1 is recruitment.** Message P01–P07 before anything else. They were interviewed about the *problem space* in PA2 and have never seen a prototype, so they satisfy *"no prior knowledge about the prototype you will test."* This is your warm list — use it. PA2's retro named scheduling as the root cause of your last sprint's slippage; this hour is the fix.

**Then lock the two live scenarios** before a single sheet is drawn — the report structure and
the slides both hang off this.

Settled scope from `PA3-Drawing-Progress.md`:

- **Main Scenario 1 — "Start a run without breaking music flow"**
  Alternatives: **1A / 1B / 1C**. Owner: Luân.
- **Main Scenario 2 — "Correct pace without looking at the screen"**
  Alternatives: **2A / 2B / 2C**. Owner: Duy.
- **Scenario 3 — "Control the run without stopping or mis-tapping"**
  Alternatives **3A / 3B / 3C** are descoped and kept on standby only.

This follows the PA schedule table's own `2 scenario × 3 alternatives` wording. Still state
the scoping line in `Group02-PA3-PaperProtype.pdf`: "P3 deferred; P1 and P2 carry three
alternatives each, per the PA schedule's 2 × 3."

Rest of the day: specs → draw 30 participant sheets → draw 24 storyboard frames → review 6
explanation cards. Two people draw in parallel: Luân for Scenario 1, Duy for Scenario 2.

### Day 2 — Record, then test

Morning: 6 videos (45 min each, in parallel where possible), then upload **unlisted** and
verify every link in an incognito window. A private video is an unwatchable video and R1 is
45% of the grade.

Midday: finalize the testing plan, then dry-run the full protocol on a teammate and time it. Cut whatever overruns.

Evening: **the sessions.** With two live drawing members, assume sequential sessions unless
CO is available as a note-taker/facilitator. Recalculate the protocol around 6 prototypes,
not 9.

Per participant, ~45–50 min: 5 min briefing → 5 min background questions → 6 tasks × ~5 min
→ 10 min wrap-up.

**Priority if you run out of time:** cover **2A / 2B / 2C** fully, without exception. Dr.
Duy's PA2 feedback was specifically that haptic/ambient patterns are perceptually hard and
need extensive testing, and your group's written response committed to testing them early.
Capture the **Cue Interpretation Accuracy** table (too fast / too slow / in range) for 2A
and 1C. Landing that one table is worth more at the presentation than thin coverage.

Never explain what a vibration pattern means before the participant guesses. The guess *is* the data.

Type up notes the same night.

### Day 3 — Analyse, build, rehearse

Morning: analysis from real notes → comparison table → select the best prototype with evidence, named weaknesses, and a concrete improvement.

Midday: draw and record the improved version.

Afternoon: report drafts in parallel (M1 on Paper Prototype, M2 on Formative Testing) while
CO builds slides.

Evening: rehearse to 8 minutes. Backfill the weekly report from the meetings you actually held on Days 1–3 — log them as they happen, don't reconstruct them.

## 5. Integrity line — do not cross

AI can write specs, captions, scripts, plan prose, and assemble reports from your material.

AI must **not** generate: observation notes, participant quotes, feedback, meeting minutes, or peer-review comments. Those are empirical records. Generating them is fabrication, not efficiency — and the fabricated version is exactly what a lecturer who has run these sessions himself will notice.

The original plan already drew this line correctly. Keep it.
